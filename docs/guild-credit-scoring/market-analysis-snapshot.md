## How the discussion evolved

- The initial Lovable request cited first-time-buyer leads under 620 with total scores >=19, about 16–22% across markets since October 21, plus sales reports of no application starts. Those were early reports with a different window and definition, not interchangeable with the October 1 all-scored-lead cohorts above. Total >=19 is not the actual qualified-segment rule.
- Initial options included capping total at 12, a large deduction, or smaller deductions with low down payment. Total-only capping was rejected because the live tier logic uses X/Y. Earlier X-cap examples were Purchase 12 and HELOC/Refi 7 for below 580.
- After live inspection and synthetic checks, the proposed direction became credit contributions -6 / 0 / 6 / 8 / 10 across all products and markets, with completed X floored at zero. The 8/10 higher-credit contributions already exist.
- At this stage, 580–619 qualified at less than half the 620+ Purchase rate, so one suggested holding its contribution unchanged while waiting for downstream evidence. The later [funnel attachment](funnel-evidence-2026-11-20.md) showed no pre-approvals among 589 qualified 580–619 Purchase leads, supporting renewed consideration of the modest three-point reduction.
- The later attachment also shows two below-580 Purchase pre-approvals and one under contract; it supersedes the literal early claim that none progressed. It does not prove zero funded value for future cohorts.
- The final attachment suggested stronger Purchase X caps for both sub-620 bands. The current [proposal](README.md) retains the moderate points schedule instead, pending Alan's implementation approval. A 580–619 lead can still qualify on strong urgency. Do not silently replace this with blanket exclusion.
- The largest estimated qualification-signal change is HELOC (about 26 percentage points from below-580 alone), motivating discussion of HELOC-first technical staging. All-market scope and potential staged rollout are different decisions; neither authorizes deployment.
